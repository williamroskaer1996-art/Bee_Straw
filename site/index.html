About the project

Two designers, one bee, one season.

Bee Straw started in the TU Delft course Computational Fabrication & MetaMaterials, autumn 2026. The brief: a 3D-printed nest for the red mason bee, made of 100 % PHA, that decays where it stands with no one having to remove it.

The idea

The timer lives in the soil

PHA barely wears in air, but it wears in wet soil. So the fuse sits just under the soil line, and the straw above it stays dry.

The material science

Behaviour from structure, not extra parts

A graded gyroid fuse: thick on one side, thin on the other, so it lets go in the direction you choose. Printed as toolpaths a normal slicer can't make.

Still open

What we don't know yet

Whether bees accept PHA, and how fast real soils break it down. The community helps answer both.

    DecayModel output · uncalibrated

    The fuse is a short gyroid section of the straw, just under the soil, with sheets thick on one side and thin on the other. Published PHA decay rates and your weather set how thick they are, so the straw lets go about 12 months after you install it. Real soils can be several times faster or slower until they are measured.
